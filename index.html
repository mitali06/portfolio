The Learning Journey

This is roughly how I got from writing my first programs to building retrieval and agent systems. It wasn't a straight line, but it was a fairly honest one.

Where it started Learning to think like an engineer
+

I started where most people do: writing code that mostly worked, then slowly learning why it broke. Data structures, algorithms, Git, and enough systems thinking to understand what a for-loop can actually do to a server. This is also where I picked up the habit of reading documentation instead of skipping straight to tutorials.

PythonData Structures & AlgorithmsGit / GitHubSQLOOP & Systems Design
Finding the math underneath Understanding how machines actually learn
+

Linear algebra, probability, and statistics stopped feeling abstract once I saw them as the reason a model converges or doesn't. From there I moved into classical ML: regression, trees, clustering, and learning to evaluate a model honestly before trusting it.

Linear AlgebraProbability & Statisticsscikit-learnFeature EngineeringModel Evaluation
Going deeper Building my first neural networks
+

This is where I really started to understand backpropagation, activation functions, and the shift from hand-crafted features to representations a network learns on its own. I built and broke enough CNNs and RNNs to understand why architecture choices matter just as much as data.

PyTorchCNNsRNNs / LSTMsBackpropagationModel Training at Scale
The shift to language Learning how LLMs actually work
+

I dug into attention mechanisms, tokenization, and how foundation models actually behave under the hood, then moved into the practical side: prompting, embeddings, and working with frontier model APIs instead of training from scratch.

Transformer ArchitectureHugging FacePrompt EngineeringEmbeddingsOpenAI / Anthropic APIs
Where I am now Actively building Building systems that retrieve, reason, and act
+

This is where everything before it comes together. I'm currently working through retrieval pipelines, vector search, agentic workflows, and evaluation methodology, mostly by implementing them in real systems rather than just reading papers and docs. It's the phase I'm in right now, and probably will be for a while. The projects below are where that learning gets tested against actual code.
